International Relations & Global Geopolitics

16th India–Japan Annual Summit

CORE FACTS: Japanese PM Sanae Takaichi made her first official visit to India (1–3 July 2026) for the 16th India–Japan Annual Summit, hosted by PM Modi in New Delhi (2 July). Joint Statement: "Advancing a Partnership of Strategic Convergence and Trust for Shared Growth, Prosperity and Resilience."

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Deepened the Special Strategic and Global Partnership. Flagship outcomes: India–Japan

Cooperative Biogas for Growth Initiative (supports India's target of 1,000 biogas plants) and reaffirmation of the

Odisha Green Hydrogen and Clean Ammonia Project. Defence track referenced ACSA and exercises JIMEX, Dharma Guardian, Malabar; Quad coordination on maritime security, critical minerals and HADR reaffirmed.

Subianto, an address to Indonesia's People's Consultative Assembly (MPR) — an honour previously extended only to Xi Jinping and Saudi King Salman — and inauguration of the restoration of the UNESCO-listed Prambanan Temple complex, Yogyakarta.

16th India–Japan Annual Summit

BACKGROUND: India and Japan have held Annual Summits since 2006, the institutional backbone of their Special Strategic and Global Partnership (elevated in 2014). This was the first meeting with Japan's new PM, making it a signal of policy continuity after a change in Japanese leadership.

CORE FACTS: Japanese PM Sanae Takaichi made her first official visit to India (1–3 July 2026) for the 16th India–Japan Annual Summit, hosted by PM Modi in New Delhi (2 July). Joint Statement: "Advancing a Partnership of Strategic Convergence and Trust for Shared Growth, Prosperity and Resilience."

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Flagship outcomes: the India–Japan Cooperative Biogas for Growth Initiative (supports India's target of 1,000 biogas plants, feeding into rural clean-energy and waste-management goals) and reaffirmation of the

Odisha Green Hydrogen and Clean Ammonia Project, part of India's National Green Hydrogen Mission. On defence, both sides referenced the Acquisition and Cross-Servicing Agreement (ACSA) and continuing exercises JIMEX, Dharma Guardian and Malabar; Quad coordination on maritime security, critical minerals and HADR was reaffirmed, since Japan and India are two of the Quad's four members alongside the US and Australia.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — remember the Biogas Initiative's numeric target (1,000 plants) and that this is the 16th summit in the annual series. Mains (GS-II, IR) — usable as an example of India sustaining bilateral institutions across a change in the partner country's leadership.

India–Indonesia State Visit & Comprehensive Strategic Partnership

BACKGROUND: India and Indonesia, the world's two largest democracies by population after each other's near- neighbours, share civilisational links (the Ramayana tradition in Java, historic Hindu-Buddhist heritage) that this visit deliberately invoked through the Prambanan Temple restoration and the "Tagore-Dewantara" framing.

CORE FACTS: PM Modi's State Visit to Indonesia (6–8 July 2026) included bilateral talks with President Prabowo

Subianto, an address to Indonesia's People's Consultative Assembly (MPR) — an honour previously extended only to Xi Jinping and Saudi King Salman, underlining the weight Jakarta placed on the visit — and inauguration of the restoration

of the UNESCO-listed Prambanan Temple complex, Yogyakarta.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Agreement to deepen cooperation in defence manufacturing, cyber security, space, critical minerals, clean energy and local-currency trade (reducing dollar dependence in bilateral settlement, a theme also visible in the Indonesia leg discussed under BRICS de-dollarization). New connectivity initiatives include development of Indonesia's Sabang Port (strategically located near the Malacca Strait mouth), cross-border QR code payments, and digital public infrastructure cooperation based on India's ONDC model — effectively an export of India's DPI stack. Both sides will mark

2026–27 as the "Tagore–Dewantara Year" of cultural and educational diplomacy (referencing Rabindranath Tagore and Indonesian education reformer Ki Hajar Dewantara), and welcomed a proposal to open an IIM Bengaluru campus in

Indonesia. Both reaffirmed a free/open Indo-Pacific, UNSC reform, and coordination via BRICS, ASEAN, G20 and IORA.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — Sabang Port's strategic location (near the Malacca Strait) and the "Tagore-Dewantara Year" naming are classic one-line-fact traps. Mains (GS-II) — a strong example for essays on India's "Act East" and DPI-diplomacy (exporting ONDC/UPI-style systems as soft power).

India–Australia Third Annual Summit

BACKGROUND: The India–Australia Annual Summit mechanism began only in 2020 after the relationship was upgraded to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership — making this the third meeting of a still-young but fast-deepening bilateral track, closely tied to Quad cooperation.

CORE FACTS: PM Modi visited Australia (8–10 July 2026) for the Third India–Australia Annual Summit in Melbourne, with

bilateral talks with PM Anthony Albanese.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Adopted a Joint Declaration on Defence and Security Cooperation; launched the India– Australia Maritime Security Collaboration Roadmap; established a Partnership on Cyber, Critical Technologies

and Supply Chains (PACTS) and an Annual Defence Ministers' Dialogue — institutionalising what had earlier been ad hoc engagement. Both sides reaffirmed commitment to conclude CECA (Comprehensive Economic Cooperation Agreement, the next stage after the existing ECTA/interim trade pact); unveiled a Roadmap for Sports Collaboration ahead of the 2030 Commonwealth Games (Ahmedabad) and 2032 Brisbane Olympics — two consecutive Commonwealth/Olympic events hosted by the two countries. Australia reiterated support for India's permanent UNSC membership.

Note the sequence: Japan (1–3 July) → Indonesia (6–8 July) → Australia (8–10 July) — a single Indo-Pacific diplomatic swing worth remembering as one block for Prelims-style "match the visit with the outcome" questions.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — CECA vs ECTA distinction (interim pact already in force; CECA is the fuller agreement still being negotiated). Mains (GS-II) — useful to illustrate how Quad members are building a lattice of bilateral pacts (PACTS, Maritime Roadmap) alongside the multilateral Quad framework.

President Murmu's Three-Nation Eastern Europe Tour

BACKGROUND: Indian presidential visits are rare and largely ceremonial/diplomatic in character (foreign policy execution rests with the PM/MEA), so a presidential tour signals a long-horizon relationship-building gesture rather than a transactional one — especially notable here since none of these three countries had received a standalone Indian presidential visit before, or in Romania's case, not for three decades.

CORE FACTS: President Droupadi Murmu undertook a State visit to Moldova, North Macedonia and Romania (19–25 July 2026) — the first-ever bilateral visit by an Indian President to Moldova and North Macedonia, and the first State visit to Romania by an Indian President in over three decades.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Talks focused on trade, investment, IT, agriculture, pharmaceuticals, food processing, tourism and people-to-people exchanges — part of India's expanding outreach to Eastern Europe, a region where India's diplomatic and economic presence has historically lagged behind Western Europe.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — the "firsts" (first bilateral visit to Moldova/N. Macedonia; first Romania visit in 30+ years) are the testable specifics; know which country got which superlative.

India–UK CETA Enters Into Force

BACKGROUND: India–UK trade talks had run for years through multiple rounds before conclusion; CETA's entry into force in July 2026 makes the UK one of the few major economies with a comprehensive trade pact with India, alongside the UAE and Australia (ECTA).

CORE FACTS: The India–UK Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA) took effect on 15 July 2026,

giving nearly 99% of Indian exports duty-free access to the UK market. It includes a Social Security Agreement exempting Indian professionals on temporary UK postings from double social security contributions for up to five years — a long-standing Indian ask, since Indian workers in the UK were previously paying into both countries' systems without benefit. On day one, over 500 export consignments worth more than USD 140 million were cleared under the new preferential regime.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Alongside the trade pact, the UK agreed to integrate IAF instructors into RAF training — economic and defence tracks advancing together, a pattern also visible in the Indonesia and Australia visits this month.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — the 99% duty-free access figure and the Social Security Agreement's 5-year exemption window are the two most quotable numbers. Mains (GS-III, Economy) — use as a case study of "new-generation" FTAs bundling services/labour- mobility provisions, not just goods tariffs.

UK Leadership Change — Andy Burnham Becomes PM

BACKGROUND: Under the UK's Westminster system, a change in the ruling party's leader can produce a new Prime Minister without a general election, since the PM is whoever commands confidence in the House of Commons — the same mechanism that has produced several UK PM transitions in the 2020s.

CORE FACTS: Andy Burnham took office as UK Prime Minister after being elected Labour Party leader, formally invited by King Charles III to form a government following the resignation of Keir Starmer.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Burnham pledged a "circuit-breaker" moment for British politics, focused on national unity and rebuilding public trust — relevant background for any GS-II comparative-governance question on parliamentary systems and how they handle mid-term leadership change without dissolving the legislature.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — simply "who is the current UK PM" is a recurring current-affairs staple; update your notes to Burnham.

Attack on MV Golden Leo Near Odesa

BACKGROUND: Black Sea shipping lanes near Ukrainian ports have remained a conflict-adjacent risk zone since the Russia-Ukraine war began, with periodic attacks on commercial (not just military) vessels — this incident is part of that ongoing pattern rather than an isolated event.

CORE FACTS: On 19 July 2026, the commercial vessel MV Golden Leo was attacked while departing the Ukrainian port of Odesa. Four Indian crew members were killed and another critically injured; the vessel carried 17 crew including five Indian nationals.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: India's MEA condemned the attack and summoned Russia's Chargé d'Affaires Vladimir Ladanov, underscoring the risk to Indian seafarers from the Black Sea conflict zone — connect this with India's global seafarer footprint (see Category 3, where India is confirmed as the world's second-largest seafarer-supplying nation), since a large share of the world's commercial crews, including in conflict-adjacent waters, are Indian nationals.

EXAM ANGLE: Mains (GS-II/III) — usable in an answer on the vulnerability of India's large seafaring workforce to geopolitical conflict zones, and the state's diplomatic tools (démarche/summoning envoys) for protecting citizens abroad.

14-Nation Maritime Defence Coalition for West Asian Sea Routes

BACKGROUND: The Bab el-Mandeb Strait, Red Sea and Gulf of Aden corridor carries a large share of Europe-Asia trade and has seen repeated attacks on merchant shipping in recent years, prompting a series of ad hoc international naval responses before this more formal coalition.

CORE FACTS: Saudi Arabia and 13 other countries — Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, Egypt, Jordan, Yemen, Nigeria, Sudan, Djibouti, Somalia, Turkey, Pakistan and Bangladesh — launched a Multinational Maritime Defence Coalition to protect

shipping through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, Red Sea and Gulf of Aden. Saudi Arabia is the founding state and permanent headquarters.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: A direct response to escalating attacks on merchant shipping in the region — the same threat environment behind India's DGMA advisory on the Strait of Hormuz (see Category 4). Note that India is not a member of this particular coalition, even though these routes are critical to Indian trade — India instead relies on independent naval deployments (e.g. anti-piracy patrols) in the wider region.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — know the coalition is Saudi-led/headquartered and note that Pakistan and Bangladesh are members but India is not, a common "who's in/who's out" distractor.

Four New Sites Added to UNESCO World Heritage List

National News & Governance

Monsoon Session of Parliament 2026

BACKGROUND: The Monsoon Session is the second of Parliament's three annual sessions (Budget, Monsoon, Winter), traditionally used to clear pending legislative business accumulated since the Budget Session.

CORE FACTS: The Monsoon Session opened 20 July and ran to 13 August 2026 (19 sittings) — the eighth session of the 18th Lok Sabha. 28 bills were pending, with seven priority legislations listed, five of them new.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Also saw a pending bill for a single higher-education regulator (replacing UGC, AICTE, NCTE — consolidating three separate regulatory bodies into one, a long-discussed reform) and an FCRA amendment listed for consideration, plus MSME amendments tightening delayed-payment enforcement and easing arbitral award enforcement, aimed at improving the ease of doing business for small enterprises.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — "8th session of the 18th Lok Sabha" is a precise, testable fact. Mains (GS-II, Polity) — the proposed single higher-education regulator is a strong example for questions on institutional consolidation and regulatory reform.

Vande Mataram Gets Statutory Protection Equal to the National Anthem

BACKGROUND: Vande Mataram has held the status of India's "National Song" (distinct from the National Anthem, Jana Gana Mana) since the Constituent Assembly's 1950 resolution, but until this amendment lacked the same statutory insult- protection that the Anthem already enjoyed under the 1971 Act.

CORE FACTS: The Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Bill, 2026 extends the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971, to Vande Mataram, making it an offence to intentionally prevent its singing or cause disturbance during rendition. Introduced in Rajya Sabha 24 July, passed Rajya Sabha 29 July, approved by Lok Sabha 30

July.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Punishable with imprisonment up to three years, a fine, or both; repeat convictions attract a minimum one-year term — mirroring the existing penalty structure for insults to the National Anthem and Flag under the same 1971 Act.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — know the distinction between "National Anthem" (Jana Gana Mana) and "National Song" (Vande Mataram) and that this Bill closes a legal-protection gap between the two, rather than changing either one's constitutional status.

Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026

BACKGROUND: The original Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024, was enacted after repeated high- profile paper-leak scandals affecting national recruitment exams; this 2026 amendment strengthens that framework further after continued incidents.

CORE FACTS: Strengthens the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024, covering exams by UPSC,

SSC, Railway Recruitment Boards, IBPS, NTA and Central Government ministries. Introduced Lok Sabha 27 July, passed Lok Sabha 29 July, Rajya Sabha 30 July — cleared within four days, signalling political consensus on the issue.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Individual offenders: 5–10 years imprisonment, fines up to ₹50 lakh; organised exam-crime rackets (the more serious category, involving networks rather than individuals): 7–10 years, fines up to ₹10 crore. Service providers (private firms conducting exams) can be debarred up to eight years and fined up to ₹5 crore. The Bill provides for Special Fast Track Courts, Special Public Prosecutors, a Special Task Force, and mandates investigation within two months and trial within three months — a much faster timeline than ordinary criminal procedure.

Directly relevant to every aspirant: this is the legal framework now protecting the exams candidates prepare for.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — keep the two punishment tiers straight: individual (5–10 yrs / ₹50 lakh) vs organised racket (7–10 yrs / ₹10 crore). This distinction is the most likely MCQ trap.

50% SSB Constable Vacancies Reserved for Ex-Agniveers

BACKGROUND: The Agnipath scheme (2022) recruits soldiers for a four-year tenure, after which only 25% are retained in the regular forces; this reservation is part of a broader government push to create post-service employment pathways (in CAPFs, state police, etc.) for the remaining 75% of Agniveers.

CORE FACTS: MHA notified the Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB) Combatised General Duty Group 'C' Posts Recruitment Rules, 2026, reserving 50% of Constable (GD) vacancies for ex-Agniveers, exempt from the written exam, PST and PET, with age relaxation up to five years for the first batch (three years thereafter).

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims/Mains — part of a wider CAPF-level pattern of Agniveer reservation; useful for GS-III questions on defence-forces human resource policy.

Two Key Reforms for Indian Coast Guard Personnel

CORE FACTS: Women officers can now be inducted via both Short Service and Permanent Commission on par with men; the process for granting ex gratia to families of personnel missing at sea (presumed dead) has been simplified — both ahead of the Coast Guard's Golden Jubilee in 2027 (the ICG was established in 1978).

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — note the Golden Jubilee year (2027), implying ICG's founding year is 1977–78 — a common static-GK cross-link.

VB-G-RAM-G — First Installment of ₹25,863 Crore Released

BACKGROUND: MGNREGA (2005) had long been criticised for wage-payment delays and low real wages; VB-G-RAM-G is positioned as its successor under the "Viksit Bharat" branding, aiming to fix these structural issues while keeping the rural employment-guarantee model intact.

CORE FACTS: Union Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan released the first installment (Mother Sanction) of ₹25,863 crore on 5 July 2026 for the Viksit Bharat–Guarantee for Rozgar and Aajeevika Mission (Gramin) — rolled out nationwide from 1 July 2026 as the successor to MGNREGA.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Average wage rates raised by 10%; no state now has a wage rate below ₹300/day — addressing a long-standing complaint that MGNREGA wages in several states had fallen behind even state minimum-wage floors. Area Officers (Joint Secretary-level) deployed to facilitate the transition and troubleshoot implementation issues.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — VB-G-RAM-G is the direct successor scheme name to memorise; do not confuse with MGNREGA, which it has now replaced administratively (though the underlying employment-guarantee legal framework may still trace back to the MGNREGA Act unless separately re-legislated — verify this technical point if it becomes central to a Mains answer).

India's First Hydrogen-Powered Train — Flagged Off in Haryana

BACKGROUND: Indian Railways had been running hydrogen-train pilot trials for some time as part of its "Mission 2030" net-zero carbon emitter target; this launch converts that pilot into India's first operational hydrogen train.

CORE FACTS: PM Modi flagged off India's first hydrogen-powered train between Jind and Sonipat, Haryana, on 17 July 2026. A 10-coach train with a capacity of around 2,600 passengers, running on hydrogen fuel cells, emitting only water vapour.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: India joins Germany, Japan, China and the US as hydrogen-train operators — a small club of nations with operational (not just pilot-stage) hydrogen rail technology. Indian Railways plans to extend hydrogen technology to

heritage lines, including the Kalka–Shimla Railway, where zero-emission operation would also protect a UNESCO- recognised heritage corridor from diesel pollution.

Highest-priority Haryana GK fact of the month: a national "first" launched in Haryana. This pairs directly with PM Modi's same-day events in Jind and Kurukshetra, Haryana.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — the exact route (Jind–Sonipat) and India's rank (5th nation globally, after Germany/Japan/China/US) are the two hardest-to-guess specifics; memorise both cold given the Haryana relevance.

Government Tightens Rules for High-Alcohol Medicines

BACKGROUND: Medicinal formulations with high alcohol content (cough syrups, tonics) have periodically been misused for intoxication, prompting tighter regulatory scrutiny under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act framework.

CORE FACTS: The Drugs Rules, 1945 were amended so that medicinal formulations containing more than 12% v/v ethyl

alcohol in quantities exceeding 30 mL lose their earlier exemption from licensing under Schedule K, and are now brought

under Schedule H1 — sale only on a Registered Medical Practitioner's prescription, with stricter record-keeping requirements for pharmacists.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — Schedule H1 vs Schedule K distinction under the Drugs Rules, 1945 is a plausible GS-III (health policy) MCQ.

Higher Education Enrolment Reaches 4.5 Crore (AISHE Report)

BACKGROUND: AISHE is the Education Ministry's annual census-style survey of India's higher-education system, tracking enrolment, faculty, and infrastructure across universities and colleges — a key data source for GS-II education-policy answers.

CORE FACTS: The AISHE reports for 2022–23 and 2023–24 show the Gross Enrolment Ratio (18–23 age group) rising to

30% in 2023–24 (from 29.5% in 2022–23 and 23.7% in 2014–15). Female GER rose to 31.2%, now exceeding male GER; the Gender Parity Index stayed above 1.0 for the seventh straight year.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Total enrolment reached 4.50 crore (up 31.5% over the decade); female enrolment grew 42% to 2.24 crore — growing faster than overall enrolment, which is why the Gender Parity Index keeps rising. STEM enrolment: 1.02 crore, with women now 44% of STEM students, still short of parity but a marked improvement from a decade ago. Total faculty rose to 17.32 lakh (from 15.16 lakh in 2014–15); women faculty rose from 5.69 lakh to 7.78 lakh over the same period.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — GER trend (23.7% in 2014-15 → 30% in 2023-24) is the single most quoted AISHE statistic; also remember GPI has been above 1.0 for seven consecutive years, meaning female GER now exceeds male GER.

I&B Ministry Launches 'Gems of India Challenge'

CORE FACTS: Pilot phase launched on the MyWAVES platform (WAVES OTT) to identify grassroots digital creators, covering Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Arunachal Pradesh, Chandigarh, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, Goa, and

Ladakh — a deliberate choice of smaller UTs/states for the pilot before a national rollout. Entries open 1–31 August 2026.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: District-level awards of ₹50,000 (up to 20 creators/district), state-level ₹2 lakh, national awards ₹5 lakh — a tiered incentive structure designed to surface talent from the district level upward rather than only rewarding already-prominent creators.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — note which specific states/UTs are covered in the pilot phase; this list (rather than the prize amounts) is the more likely factual test.

Economy, Banking & Finance

RBI Monetary Policy Stance Through July 2026

BACKGROUND: The RBI's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) meets bi-monthly (six times a year) to set the repo rate; July fell in the gap between the June and August review cycles, so no fresh decision was taken this month — the rate simply carried over from the prior review.

CORE FACTS: Repo rate held at 5.25% (neutral stance) through July, following the December 2025 cut. SDF at 5.0%, MSF/Bank Rate at 5.50%. No fresh MPC meeting fell within July; the next bi-monthly review was due in early August 2026.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: GDP growth forecasts were revised through the year — a mid-year (June 2026) review had cut the FY27 forecast to 6.6% (from 6.9%) and raised the FY27 CPI inflation forecast to 5.1%, citing West Asia tensions, crude prices, and rupee pressure — a reminder that domestic monetary policy is increasingly shaped by external geopolitical shocks.

Source: RBI press releases confirm the 5.25% repo rate; some aggregator sites show stale figures.

before using it as a high-stakes exam fact.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — know the full policy-rate corridor (SDF 5.0% / Repo 5.25% / MSF-Bank Rate 5.50%), not just the headline repo figure; corridor-width questions are a recurring RBI-policy MCQ format.

Eighth WTO Trade Policy Review of India

BACKGROUND: The WTO's Trade Policy Review Mechanism periodically examines each member's trade policies for transparency; India, as a larger trading economy, is reviewed more frequently than smaller members.

CORE FACTS: The WTO completed the eighth review of India's trade policy under the Trade Policy Review (TPR) Mechanism, concluding in Geneva on 23 July 2026. 68 WTO members participated; 44 submitted 1,094 written

questions. India's delegation was led by Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agarwal.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — "eighth review" and the WTO body's full name (Trade Policy Review Mechanism) are the testable specifics.

India's Merchandise Exports Grow 15% Despite Global Disruptions

CORE FACTS: Merchandise exports grew 15% during April–July of the current financial year despite the Red Sea crisis,

Strait of Hormuz uncertainty and energy-market volatility, per Commerce & Industry Minister Piyush Goyal.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: The figure is notable precisely because it came against a backdrop of active shipping-route disruption (see the DGMA Hormuz advisory and the 14-nation maritime coalition elsewhere in this compendium) — suggesting Indian exporters and logistics networks have found workarounds rather than seeing volumes collapse.

EXAM ANGLE: Mains (GS-III) — pair this figure with the Hormuz/Red Sea disruption entries to build an answer on India's trade resilience amid geopolitical shipping risk.

India's First Telecom Manufacturing Zone — Gwalior

CORE FACTS: MoU signed 30 July 2026 between the Department of Telecommunications and the Government of Madhya

Pradesh to set up India's first Telecom Manufacturing Zone (TMZ) at Gwalior, under Make in India and Atmanirbhar Bharat.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: A dedicated TMZ is a new category of industrial zone distinct from generic SEZs — purpose-built for telecom equipment, a sector India still imports heavily in (base stations, networking gear), making this part of the broader import-substitution push alongside PLI schemes.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — "India's first TMZ, Gwalior, MP" is a clean, testable static fact once it appears in a compilation like this.

Air India's 'Easy Connect' — Amritsar Hub

CORE FACTS: Air India launched a hub-and-spoke service at Amritsar, connecting the city to 27 international destinations across North America, the UK, Europe and Australia via seamless domestic–international transfers — aimed at improving connectivity for travellers from Punjab, Jammu & Kashmir and Himachal Pradesh, who previously had to route through Delhi for most international connections.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — note Amritsar (not Chandigarh) as the hub city; a plausible state-GK distractor for Punjab/Haryana- region aspirants.

India Crosses One Billion Mark in Social Protection Coverage

BACKGROUND: The ILO's global social-protection tracking (linked to SDG Goal 1.3) had long flagged India's low coverage; this milestone reflects the cumulative reach of schemes like Ayushman Bharat, PM-JAY, EPFO/ESIC, and various pension and insurance schemes counted together.

CORE FACTS: Coverage rose to 68.4% in 2026, up from 19% in 2015, validated by the ILO. Announced 16 July by Labour

and Employment Minister Mansukh Mandaviya.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: A more than three-and-a-half-fold increase in coverage over roughly a decade, reflecting cumulative expansion of welfare schemes; strengthens India's positioning in international social-security/SDG comparisons.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — the two anchor figures (19% in 2015 → 68.4% in 2026) and the validating body (ILO) are the testable elements.

Modified UDAN Scheme — 120 New Destinations

BACKGROUND: UDAN ("Ude Desh ka Aam Naagrik," launched 2017) subsidises regional air connectivity to underserved routes; this is its next phase, extending the model with longer funding support.

CORE FACTS: Civil Aviation Minister K. Ram Mohan Naidu unveiled the next UDAN phase (17 July), targeting 120 new destinations and 4 crore additional passenger journeys over 10 years. Extends viability-gap funding from three to five

years; outlay ₹29,000 crore; plans for 100 more airports and 200 heliports.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — the extension of viability-gap funding from 3 to 5 years is the specific policy change most likely to be tested (versus just the headline destination-count figure).

New Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme (MPMS)

BACKGROUND: The original PLI scheme for large-scale electronics (2020) helped make India a major mobile-assembly hub; MPMS is designed to push the industry from assembly toward deeper local value addition and design capability, addressing a common criticism that PLI-era manufacturing remained mostly low-value assembly.

CORE FACTS: Union Cabinet approved the MPMS on 15 July, with a budget outlay of ₹62,500 crore, replacing the earlier PLI scheme for large-scale electronics manufacturing.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Offers additional incentives of up to 1.5% for local value addition and 3% for product design/R&D by Indian brands — explicitly rewarding design and component sourcing rather than just final assembly; a five-year scheme targeting around 60,000 direct jobs.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — MPMS replaces the electronics PLI scheme; don't confuse the two when a question asks which scheme is "currently" operative for mobile manufacturing.

Semicon 2.0 — End-to-End Semiconductor Ecosystem

BACKGROUND: Semicon 1.0 (India Semiconductor Mission, 2021) focused on attracting fabrication and packaging investment; Semicon 2.0 widens the ambition to the full value chain, recognising that a semiconductor ecosystem needs design and R&D talent, not just factories.

Andhra Pradesh's 'Pillale Sampada' Scheme

BACKGROUND: Several southern states adopted two-child-norm policies decades ago to control population growth; AP's reversal here reflects growing anxiety in low-fertility southern states about the political and fiscal consequences of an ageing, shrinking population base relative to higher-fertility northern states.

CORE FACTS: On World Population Day (11 July 2026), AP CM N. STRATEGIC IMPACT: Reflects the North–South demographic divergence debate ahead of post-2026 delimitation — southern states with lower Total Fertility Rates (AP's TFR ~1.7 per NFHS-5, below the national 2.0 and replacement level of 2.1) fear losing parliamentary seats under population-based delimitation, since seats are apportioned by population and the South has controlled its population growth more successfully than the North.

EXAM ANGLE: Mains (GS-I/II, Population & Polity) — a high-value example connecting demography (TFR figures), welfare policy (cash incentives), and constitutional politics (delimitation) in one current event.

Iron & Steel Sector Brought Under the Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS)

BACKGROUND: CCTS is India's domestic carbon market mechanism, first covering sectors like aluminium, cement and chlor-alkali; steel's inclusion extends the scheme to one of the highest-emitting industrial sectors, a necessary step if the scheme is to meaningfully affect India's overall emissions trajectory.

CORE FACTS: The MoEFCC issued a draft notification (dated 26 June 2026, widely reported through early July) proposing GHG emission-intensity targets for 255 iron and steel units under the CCTS, inserting a new Third Schedule into the

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Steel contributes 10–12% of India's total GHG emissions; required emission-intensity cuts range 2.1–9.3% (median ~5.5%) across entities, part of the Steel Ministry's goal of 2.2 tonnes CO2/tonne of crude steel by 2030, feeding into India's Net Zero 2070 target. CCTS now covers nine major industrial sectors, making steel's inclusion a significant expansion of coverage.

Note the date carefully: the notification is dated 26 June 2026 but was widely covered by Indian press through the first two weeks of July — treat the underlying policy as a July current-affairs item with a late-June origin, not a purely July-dated one.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — steel's 10–12% share of India's total GHG emissions is the key comparative statistic; Mains (GS-III, Environment) — strong example for a Net Zero 2070 / market-based climate-policy answer.

Science, Defense & Technology

Vikram-1 — India's First Privately Developed Orbital Launch Vehicle

BACKGROUND: India opened its space sector to private players in 2020 (creation of IN-SPACe as the regulatory/promotional body); Skyroot Aerospace has since been one of the leading private launch-vehicle startups, having earlier flown the sub-orbital Vikram-S (2022) as a technology demonstrator.

CORE FACTS: On 18 July 2026, Hyderabad-based startup Skyroot Aerospace achieved a major milestone under Mission Agaman, successfully placing payloads into a 450-km low Earth orbit — India's first privately developed orbital launch, from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Vikram-1 is a three-stage solid-propellant rocket with a liquid-fuelled Raman engine for precise orbital adjustment. Marks India's entry into the league of nations with proven private orbital launch capability — moving beyond the earlier sub-orbital demonstration (Vikram-S) to a fully operational orbital vehicle, and validating India's private-space-sector liberalisation policy.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — distinguish Vikram-S (2022, sub-orbital demo) from Vikram-1 (2026, first private orbital launch) — a classic "don't confuse the predecessor with the milestone" trap.

India's First Indigenous Turbojet Engine

BACKGROUND: Jet-engine technology has historically been one of India's biggest defence-manufacturing gaps, with even indigenous aircraft platforms (like the Tejas) relying on imported engines; this indigenous turbojet is a step toward closing that gap, even if only at the smaller 350 kg thrust class rather than full fighter-engine scale.

CORE FACTS: India developed its first indigenous expendable turbojet engine in the 350 kg thrust class, designed by

DRDO's Gas Turbine Research Establishment (GTRE) and manufactured by Hyderabad-based Azad Engineering.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — note this is an "expendable" engine (used in missiles/drones, single-use) at 350 kg thrust — not to be confused with reusable fighter-aircraft engines, which remain a separate, larger challenge.

Kusha Long-Range Surface-to-Air Missile — Maiden Flight Test

BACKGROUND: Kusha is being developed as part of India's broader push for an indigenous layered air-defence shield, complementing existing systems like the Akash and the imported S-400, reducing dependence on foreign air-defence imports over time.

CORE FACTS: DRDO conducted the maiden flight test of the long-range surface-to-air missile (LRSAM) Kusha on 23 July 2026, from Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam Island off the Odisha coast.

STRATEGIC IMPACT: Designed to neutralise a wide spectrum of aerial threats — fighter aircraft, cruise missiles, UAVs and large enemy aircraft across extended ranges — a cornerstone of India's future layered air defence, reducing import dependence on systems like the Russian S-400.

EXAM ANGLE: Prelims — test-site name (Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam Island, Odisha) is frequently reused across multiple DRDO missile tests — a good site-name to have memorised generally, not just for Kusha.

Pinaka Long Range Guided Rocket — Flight-Tested

✎High-Yield Quiz — 15 MCQs (July 2026)

Q1. Skyroot Aerospace's Vikram-1, launched 18 July 2026, is significant because it is:

A) India's first reusable rocket B) India's first privately developed orbital launch vehicle C) ISRO's first crewed mission D) The first Indian rocket launched from a foreign spaceport

Answer: B. Vikram-1 placed payloads into a 450-km LEO from Sriharikota — India's first privately developed orbital launch, under Mission Agaman.

Q2. India's first hydrogen-powered train was flagged off between which two towns?

A) Kalka–Shimla B) Jind–Sonipat C) Panipat–Karnal D) Rohtak–Hisar

Answer: B. PM Modi flagged it off between Jind and Sonipat, Haryana, on 17 July 2026 — a 10-coach, zero-emission train.

Q3. The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026 sets the punishment for organised exam- crime rackets at:

A) 2–5 years, fine up to ₹10 lakh B) 5–10 years, fine up to ₹50 lakh C) 7–10 years, fine up to ₹10 crore D) Life imprisonment

Answer: C. Individual offenders face 5–10 years/₹50 lakh; organised rackets face 7–10 years and fines up to ₹10 crore.

Q4. Which bill gave "Vande Mataram" the same statutory protection as the National Anthem?

A) National Symbols Act, 2026 B) Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Bill, 2026 C) Constitution (Amendment) Bill D) Indian Penal Code Amendment

Answer: B. It extends the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971, to cover Vande Mataram.

Q5. The DRDO's Kusha missile, flight-tested on 23 July 2026, is a:

A) Submarine-launched ballistic missile B) Long-range surface-to-air missile (LRSAM) C) Anti-tank guided missile D) Hypersonic cruise missile

Answer: B. Kusha is a long-range surface-to-air missile designed to counter fighter aircraft, cruise missiles, UAVs and large enemy aircraft.

Q6. Spain's win over Argentina in the FIFA World Cup 2026 final made it the first nation to:

A) Win three consecutive World Cups B) Hold both men's and women's FIFA World Cup titles simultaneously C) Win the World Cup without conceding a goal D) Host and win the World Cup in the same year

Answer: B. Spain became the first nation to simultaneously hold both titles, also conceding only one goal in the entire tournament.

Q7. The VB-G-RAM-G scheme, whose first installment of ₹25,863 crore was released on 5 July 2026, replaced which earlier programme?

A) PMAY-Gramin B) MGNREGA C) NRLM D) PM-KISAN

Answer: B. The Viksit Bharat-Guarantee for Rozgar and Aajeevika Mission (Gramin) rolled out nationwide from 1 July 2026, transitioning from MGNREGA.

Q8. PV Sindhu's Japan Open 2026 title, her first in over two years, was won at the expense of which player in the final?

A) Chen Yufei B) Tai Tzu-ying C) Akane Yamaguchi D) An Se-young

Answer: C. Sindhu beat Akane Yamaguchi 21-17, 21-17; she had beaten Chen Yufei in the semifinal.

Q9. The 14-nation Maritime Defence Coalition formed in July 2026 to secure the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, Red Sea and Gulf of Aden is headquartered in and founded by:

A) Egypt B) UAE C) Saudi Arabia D) Bangladesh

Answer: C. Saudi Arabia is the founding state and permanent headquarters of the coalition, which includes 13 other nations.

Q10. Semicon 2.0, approved on 15 July 2026, differs from Semicon 1.0 mainly by:

A) Focusing only on chip fabrication plants B) Providing end-to-end support across six areas beyond manufacturing incentives C) Being limited to talent development D) Replacing the PLI scheme for mobile phones

Answer: B. Semicon 2.0 (₹1.27 lakh crore) expands support to chip design, materials/equipment, fabrication, packaging, R&D and talent development. (Option D describes the separate MPMS scheme.)

Q11. At the 72nd National Film Awards, the Best Actor award was jointly shared by Mammootty and which other actor?

A) Rajkumar Periasamy B) Kartik Aaryan C) Vicky Kaushal D) Ranveer Singh

Q12. India's first Telecom Manufacturing Zone (TMZ), agreed via MoU on 30 July 2026, will be located at:

A) Noida, Uttar Pradesh B) Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh C) Bengaluru, Karnataka D) Indore, Madhya Pradesh

Answer: B. The MoU was signed between the Department of Telecommunications and the Government of Madhya Pradesh for a TMZ at Gwalior.

Q13. The Third India–Australia Annual Summit (July 2026) established which new dialogue mechanism, alongside the PACTS partnership?

A) Annual Defence Ministers' Dialogue B) Quad Foreign Ministers' Forum C) Indo-Pacific Trade Council D) Bilateral Space Council

Answer: A. Alongside launching the Maritime Security Collaboration Roadmap and PACTS (Cyber, Critical Technologies and Supply Chains), the summit established an Annual Defence Ministers' Dialogue.

Q14. India became the world's second-largest supplier of seafarers in 2026, according to which report — behind only which country?

A) IMO Report; behind China B) BIMCO-ICS Seafarer Workforce Report; behind the Philippines C) UNCTAD Report; behind Indonesia D) ILO Report; behind Russia

Answer: B. Per the BIMCO-ICS Seafarer Workforce Report 2026, India supplies 311,936 maritime professionals (12.16% of the global workforce), ranking behind only the Philippines.

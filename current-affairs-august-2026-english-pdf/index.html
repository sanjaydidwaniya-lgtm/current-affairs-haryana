1. National Affairs & Independence Day Special

India celebrated its 80th Independence Day on 15 August 2026. PM Narendra Modi delivered his 13th consecutive address from the Red Fort — the second-longest unbroken run after Jawaharlal Nehru's seventeen. The theme was “Yuva Shakti – Leading the Journey to Viksit Bharat@2047”, coinciding with 150 years of Vande Mataram; for the first time in 80 years, the full version of the song was played at the Red Fort by the Army band.

  • PM Modi announced a target of 100 GW nuclear power capacity by 2047 and 5 new reactors this decade.
  • Free AI skill training for 1 crore youth within one year, plus free online coaching for competitive exams, was announced.
  • A nationwide sports talent hunt for children aged 5–15 years and a new Civil Defence network were unveiled.
  • Target of creating 6 crore ‘Lakhpati Didis’ under the rural women livelihood mission.
  • PM Modi unveiled ‘Shakti Ki Saptadhara’ — a 7-pronged strategy spanning manufacturing, agriculture, technology, infrastructure, defence, green economy and soft power.

Key Bills & Legislative Action

  • Kerala Alteration of Name Bill, 2026 passed by Lok Sabha, renaming the state ‘Keralam’.
  • Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Bill, 2026 extends legal protection to Vande Mataram on par with the National Anthem.
  • Rajya Sabha passed the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Bill, 2026, raising sanctioned SC strength from 34 to 38.
  • Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Bill, 2026 makes a Judicial Magistrate's order mandatory for registrations delayed beyond two years.
  • MHA notified the Immigration and Foreigners (Amendment) Rules, 2026; India added 11 new e-visa entry points, taking the total to 88.

Infrastructure, Governance & Welfare

  • PM Modi inaugurated the Alluri Sitarama Raju International Airport, Bhogapuram (Andhra Pradesh) on 1 August; commercial ops began 17 August.
  • CCEA approved the Rs.8,970.20 crore Guwahati–Tezpur four-lane corridor (NH-15, Assam).
  • PPPAC gave in-principle approval for private operation of 11 airports across 5 bundles (Amritsar, Varanasi, Bhubaneswar, Raipur, Tirupati); Rs.8,622 crore investment over a 50-year concession.
  • HAL Airport, Bengaluru operationalised India's first satellite-guided helicopter approach (RNP CAT H).
  • Pradhan Mantri Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana crossed 1 crore household registrations.
  • Home Minister Amit Shah announced 200+ arrests in the ISI-backed ‘Shahzad Bhatti Network’ ahead of Independence Day.
  • Operation Trishul: India has brought back 274 fugitives from 36 countries since 2019.
  • Government blocked 3,718 fraudulent apps, deactivated 15.75 lakh SIM cards and 5.77 lakh IMEIs linked to cyber fraud.
  • August Kranti Diwas (8 Aug, 84th anniversary of Quit India Movement) and Partition Horrors Remembrance Day (14 Aug) were observed nationwide.

Monsoon Session 2026 – Parliament Wrap-up

The Monsoon Session ran from 20 July to 13 August 2026. Of 12 bills introduced, 11 were passed.

  • Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Act, 2026 received assent on 11 August, raising sanctioned strength to 38, applied retrospectively from 16 May 2026.
  • Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026 (passed 10 Aug) creates a National Tribunals Commission for tribunal appointments and uniformity.
  • Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026 (passed 13 Aug) restricts state powers to levy certain taxes on mineral-bearing lands.
  • Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026 and the National Co-operative Development Corporation (Amendment) Bill, 2026 were also passed.

Judicial Developments

  • Union Cabinet approved a permanent Bench of the High Court of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh, to be located in Ladakh (20 Aug).
  • A 9-judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court delivered its judgment on the meaning of “industry” under Section 2(j) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 (20 Aug).
  • Follow-up directions issued for raising judicial officers' retirement age to 62 years in several states.

Independence Day 2026 honours: 1,057 Gallantry and Service Medals were announced, per Employment News — comprising 301 Medals for Gallantry (272 to police, 29 to fire services), 92 President's Medals for Distinguished Service and 664 Medals for Meritorious Service.

4. International Affairs

Abelardo de la Espriella sworn in as 36th President of Colombia. Colombian lawyer and conservative politician Abelardo de la Espriella was sworn in as the 36th President of Colombia on 7 August 2026, succeeding outgoing President Gustavo Petro. His inauguration marked a political shift in Bogotá after a tightly contested election, and is being watched closely for its effect on Colombia's relations with neighbouring Venezuela and on US–Latin America policy more broadly.

Devastating earthquake strikes Colombia. A powerful 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck Colombia during the month, killing more than 132 people and ranking among the deadliest natural disasters recorded anywhere in the world in 2026. The scale of destruction added pressure on the newly sworn-in Espriella government, which had to coordinate an emergency disaster-response effort within days of taking office.

Iran war and Strait of Hormuz crisis continue. The 2026 Iran war, which began on 28 February 2026, and the resulting Strait of Hormuz crisis remained unresolved through August. Iran indicated it would reopen the strait to normal shipping only if the United States lifted sanctions and halted military action against it. The state oil company ADNOC reported that 15 of its vessels had been attacked in the strait since the war began. Against this backdrop, the US launched ‘Operation Economic Outcast’ to intensify economic pressure on Tehran; separately, US President Trump announced what he called an “Economic D-Day” against Iran, calling on allied nations to join a coordinated economic isolation campaign. Iran and Oman continued parallel negotiations on how to manage shipping traffic through the strait safely, even as, late in the month, US forces carried out limited strikes on Iranian rocket launchers positioned on Larak Island. The strategic stakes remain enormous: the Strait of Hormuz is estimated to carry 20–25% of the world's seaborne oil trade, so any prolonged disruption has direct consequences for global energy prices, including in India.

Mecca Joint Defence Agreement signed. Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan signed the trilateral Mecca Joint Defence Agreement on 7 August 2026, a pact that includes a mutual-defence clause explicitly modelled on NATO's Article 5 — meaning an armed attack on any one signatory is to be treated as an attack on all three. Analysts view the agreement as a notable realignment of West Asian and South Asian security architecture, deepening military and defence-industrial ties between Riyadh, Ankara and Islamabad outside traditional Western-led security frameworks; it carries strategic implications for India along its western maritime and land flanks given Pakistan's central role in the pact.

PCA rules Indus Waters Treaty remains binding. The Permanent Court of Arbitration ruled on 31 August 2026 that the Indus Waters Treaty of 1960 remains fully binding on both India and Pakistan, rejecting the argument that India's earlier decision to place the treaty “in abeyance” could unilaterally suspend its legal force. Pakistan welcomed the ruling as a vindication of its position, while India rejected the PCA's jurisdiction over the matter altogether. The ruling reaffirms the treaty's legal continuity and limits either party's ability to walk away from it unilaterally, with significant implications for future water-sharing diplomacy between the two countries.

Other notable international developments. Uzbekistan and Azerbaijan signed a Treaty of Eternal Friendship on 23 August 2026, deepening cooperation between the two Central Asian and South Caucasus states. Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi visited South Korea on 19–20 August as part of continuing efforts to revive multiparty talks on the Korean Peninsula. The Armenia–Azerbaijan peace process marked the first anniversary of the Washington Peace Summit on 10 August, a milestone in the two countries' gradual normalisation of relations.

On the defence-cooperation front, India and Malaysia held the third edition of their joint air exercise ‘Udara Shakti 2026’ at Subang Air Base between 10 and 14 August, while an India–Maldives ‘Favara–UPI’ cross-border payment corridor was launched to enable real-time remittances via UPI between the two countries. On the sporting and cultural front, Ahmedabad was officially confirmed as host of the 2030 Commonwealth Games at the closing ceremony of the Glasgow 2026 Games — India's second time hosting the event after Delhi 2010 — and the 48th UNESCO World Heritage Committee session, held in Busan, South Korea, inscribed one Indian site among 25 new World Heritage properties. 5. Economy, Banking & Business

RBI holds repo rate at 5.25% for a fourth straight meeting. The Reserve Bank of India's Monetary Policy Committee met from 3 to 5 August 2026 and unanimously decided to hold the policy repo rate at 5.25% — the fourth consecutive meeting without a change — while maintaining a neutral policy stance. The Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) rate was kept at 5.00%, and both the Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) rate and the Bank Rate were held at 5.50%. Looking ahead, the RBI projected real GDP growth for FY 2026-27 at 6.7% (with quarterly estimates of 7.0% in Q1, 6.4% in Q2, 6.5% in Q3 and 6.8% in Q4), and CPI inflation at 5.0% for the year (4.7% in Q2, 5.9% in Q3 and 5.5% in Q4). On the external front, both merchandise exports and imports grew strongly in July 2026, though the trade deficit widened on the back of rising electronics imports; Foreign Portfolio Investors turned net buyers in Indian equities through July–August, and forex reserves stayed comfortable at roughly 11 months of import cover. The RBI also expanded its Upper-Layer NBFC list to 17 entities for FY27, newly bringing in public-sector companies REC, PFC, IRFC and HUDCO under this tighter regulatory category.

Key banking and business developments. Ketan Merchant received a three-month extension as interim Managing Director and CEO of Fino Payments Bank, providing continuity at the leadership level while the bank finalises a permanent appointment. The Income Tax Department set 31 August 2026 as the filing deadline for non-audit ITR-3 and ITR-4 filers, a date that draws heavy exam-relevant attention every year given its administrative significance. The Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) eased rupee-invoicing rules for exporters under the Foreign Trade Policy 2023, a move intended to reduce currency-conversion costs and encourage more exporters to invoice international trade directly in rupees. India's steel consumption grew 7.9% during April–July 2026, reflecting continued strength in construction and infrastructure activity. In a major corporate development, Larsen & Toubro, through its LTN Compute and Vyoma.AI arms, secured an order worth Rs.10,000–15,000 crore to build India's largest single-cluster NVIDIA B300 AI factory in Chennai — a landmark project for India's AI-infrastructure ambitions. Separately, a US judge dropped criminal charges against businessman Gautam Adani, closing out a legal matter that had drawn considerable market attention. The government was also reported to be weighing a Rs.2.5 lakh crore MSME credit guarantee scheme, modelled loosely on the pandemic-era ECLGS scheme, to support exporters affected by the ongoing West Asia shipping crisis.

6. Defence & Security

Agni-4 missile successfully test-fired. India successfully test-fired the Agni-4 Medium-Range Ballistic Missile from the Integrated Test Range at Chandipur, Odisha, as part of a routine user trial intended to validate the missile's reliability, accuracy and operational readiness within India's strategic deterrence arsenal.

Bhawana Kanth becomes first woman to complete FCL course. Squadron Leader Bhawana Kanth achieved a landmark for the Indian Air Force by becoming the first Indian woman fighter pilot to complete the Fighter Combat Leader (FCL) course at the Tactics and Air Combat Development Establishment (TACDE) in Gwalior — widely regarded as the IAF's equivalent of the elite “Top Gun” programme, and a major milestone in the integration of women into frontline combat roles.

DRDO missile technology transfer approved. Defence Minister Rajnath Singh approved the Transfer of Technology (ToT) of all conventional missile systems developed by the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) to Indian private defence manufacturers, a move intended to help domestic companies take these systems from development into industrial-scale production, expand indigenous manufacturing capacity, and generate employment for skilled professionals in engineering, electronics, precision manufacturing, testing and advanced defence sciences (see the related item on missile-technology transfer and defence-manufacturing jobs in Section 13.1).

Other defence and security developments. India's second indigenous Diving Support Vessel, ‘Nipun’, was delivered to the Indian Navy, strengthening the Navy's deep-sea diving and underwater intervention capabilities (see also Section 12.4 for the commissioning of the sister vessel INS Nipun class detail). 7. Science, Technology & Space

‘Param Pragya’ supercomputer inaugurated. Prime Minister Modi inaugurated ‘Param Pragya’, an AI-enabled high-performance supercomputing facility, at IIT Delhi's Sonipat campus. The facility is intended to support advanced research in artificial intelligence, scientific computing and data-intensive research across disciplines, reinforcing India's push to build sovereign high-performance computing capacity.

India's first commercial Earth-observation satellite constellation approved. IN-SPACe, India's space sector regulator, approved the country's first fully commercial Earth-observation satellite constellation under a public–private partnership model, to be built by the Allied Orbits consortium comprising PixxelSpace, Dhruva Space, PierSight and SatSure. The constellation will comprise 12 satellites, represents an investment of over Rs.1,200 crore, and is targeted to become fully operational by 2029 — a significant step in opening up India's space sector to commercial, privately-built imaging infrastructure.

Skyroot's Vikram-1 reaches orbit. Skyroot Aerospace's Vikram-1 became the first privately-built Indian rocket to successfully reach orbit, a milestone that cements Skyroot's position as India's first space ‘unicorn’, with a valuation of around US$1.1 billion. The achievement is being read as a turning point for India's private space launch industry, which has grown rapidly since the sector was opened to private players.

Other science and technology developments. ISRO held the grand finale of the Bharatiya Antariksh Hackathon (BAH) 2026 at the National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC) in Hyderabad on 6–7 August, bringing together student and start-up teams working on space-technology applications. BHASHINI, India's Digital India language-technology mission, signed a Statement of Intent with NITI Aayog to advance the use of multilingual AI in governance and public service delivery. The AYUSH Ministry signed an MoU with IndiaAI to apply artificial intelligence to research in Ayurveda, Siddha and other traditional systems of medicine. On the international front, NASA outlined its proposed SR-1 Freedom mission and a ‘SkyFall Helicopter’ concept, targeting a 2028 launch. Domestically, the National Space Technology Conclave 2026 was held on 17–18 August at Chandigarh University in collaboration with ISRO and the Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST), themed “Emerging Space Technologies for India's Future Space Missions”, with Group Captain Shubhanshu Shukla — the first Indian on the International Space Station — attending as special guest; India's space economy was estimated at roughly US$9 billion, supported by about 440 registered space start-ups as of mid-2026. On the defence technology side, the US released its National Security Science & Technology Strategy in mid-August, prioritising undersea superiority, space (including counter-space capabilities) and AI/autonomy as the top battlefield domains of the coming decade, while India continued to progress its own Space-Based Surveillance programme, which envisages 52 military satellites, alongside indigenous development of High-Altitude Pseudo-Satellites (HAPS) for long-endurance intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance.

8. Environment, Ecology & Climate Change

August 2026 was the hottest August on record. Global average surface air temperature reached 16.96°C — 1.65°C above the pre-industrial (1850–1900) baseline and 0.85°C above the 1991–2020 average, according to Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S) data. This was the first month to cross the symbolically important 1.5°C mark since November 2025, and it stands jointly as the warmest month ever recorded, tied with July 2023. Intensifying El Niño conditions are raising the risk of a deficient Indian monsoon — the IMD had projected rainfall at around 90% of the Long Period Average (LPA) — alongside heightened wildfire risk in Indonesia and extreme-heat events more broadly.

12th BRICS Environment Ministers' Meeting

India hosted the 12th BRICS Environment Ministers' Meeting in New Delhi on 18 August, under its 2026 BRICS Chairship. The meeting adopted a Joint Ministerial Statement along with four Knowledge Compendiums covering Sustainable Lifestyles, Integrated Landscape Management (addressing desertification), Forest Fire Preparedness & Response, and People-centred Climate Adaptation. Key priorities discussed included climate finance — specifically, ensuring grant and concessional funding reaches adaptation efforts in developing countries — and a shared opposition among BRICS members to unilateral trade measures such as the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), which member states view as a disguised trade barrier.

Biodiversity & Other Notes

Several notable biodiversity developments were reported during the month. New species were discovered, including Begonia neelamorum in Arunachal Pradesh and new cascade frog species from the Northeast and West Bengal. The Himalayan Serow was recorded in the Dalhousie forests for the first time in roughly 20 years, a positive sign for the region's wildlife corridors. On the climate side, Greenland's Petermann Glacier calved a massive 76.4 sq km ice island on 4 August, one of the larger calving events recorded in recent years. Domestically, the Centre formally added Heatwave and Lightning to India's notified disaster list, taking the total number of notified natural calamities to 14 — a change expected to strengthen access to disaster-response financing for heat- and lightning-related emergencies (see the fuller item on this expansion in Section 13). India also launched its first-ever Guide to Grasslands and Open Natural Ecosystems at UNCCD COP17 in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia — a document that documents the diversity, distribution and ecological importance of the country's grasslands, savannas, deserts and other open landscapes, ecosystems that support biodiversity, pastoral livelihoods and climate resilience but are often mistakenly treated as degraded land (see Section 12.5 and Section 13.1 for related coverage).

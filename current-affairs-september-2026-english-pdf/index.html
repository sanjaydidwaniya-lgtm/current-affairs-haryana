1.1 Polity, Governance and Judiciary

Supreme Court Delivers Split Verdict on CEC/EC Appointment Law23 Sep 2026

  • A two-judge bench of Justice Dipankar Datta and Justice Satish Chandra Sharma split on whether the petitions challenging the Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023 should be referred to a Constitution Bench.
  • Justice Datta held that the Act must be tested against the Anoop Baranwal (March 2023) principle that the ECI should be insulated from exclusive executive control; he observed that a 2:1 executive-majority panel fails the 'perception test' of neutrality.
  • Justice Sharma held that whether Parliament could exclude a neutral selector (the CJI) from the panel is an important question of constitutional interpretation arising for the first time, to be decided by a Constitution Bench under Article 145(3).
  • The matter goes to the Chief Justice of India, who was asked to consider constituting a five-judge bench. The 2023 Act continues to operate until the challenge is decided. Note: this was a split on referral to a larger bench, not a final ruling on the Act's validity.
  • Background: Anoop Baranwal (Mar 2023) - interim panel of PM, Leader of Opposition and CJI. The Dec 2023 Act replaced the CJI with a Union Cabinet minister nominated by the PM (Section 7). Current CEC: Gyanesh Kumar.

EXAM POINT: Article 324(2): President appoints CEC/ECs subject to a law made by Parliament. Article 145(3): minimum five judges for a substantial question of law on interpretation of the Constitution.

Ladakh: Proposed Article 371 'Chapter K' Safeguards (Proposal, Not Enacted)9 Sep 2026

  • At a Union Home Ministry sub-committee meeting in New Delhi on 9 September, the Centre proposed a new constitutional provision - reported as 'Article 371(K)' - for Ladakh, in place of Sixth Schedule status. It would cover a directly elected UT-level body and safeguards for land, culture, language, forests and environment. Reports said the talks advanced agreement on a directly elected territorial body and Article 371-type protection for land, culture, heritage, environment and natural resources, but the Leh Apex Body (LAB) and Kargil Democratic Alliance (KDA) said they remained firm on Sixth Schedule inclusion and statehood.
  • Status: a proposal under discussion; no constitutional amendment has been passed. Next round of talks was scheduled in Kargil in the first week of October. LAB and KDA still press for Sixth Schedule inclusion and statehood.

EXAM POINT: Existing Article 371 clauses: 371A (Nagaland), 371B (Assam), 371C (Manipur), 371D/E (Andhra Pradesh and Telangana), 371F (Sikkim), 371G (Mizoram), 371H (Arunachal), 371I (Goa), 371J (Karnataka). There is no enacted 371(K) - the Ladakh clause is only proposed. Ladakh became a UT on 31 Oct 2019.

Six States Sign Kishau Multipurpose Project Agreement15 Sep 2026

  • The Chief Ministers of Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Rajasthan and Delhi were among those who signed the Kishau Multipurpose Project agreement in New Delhi (six participating states).
  • Project: a 232.6-metre-high dam on the Tons River along the Uttarakhand-Himachal Pradesh border. Aims: Yamuna rejuvenation, drinking-water supply, irrigation of about 97,000 hectares and hydropower.
  • Designated a National Project: the Centre bears 90% of the water-component cost and the participating states share the remaining 10%.

EXAM POINT: Haryana is a beneficiary state. Tons is the largest tributary of the Yamuna.

18th BRICS Summit Concludes in New Delhi12-13 Sep 2026

  • India hosted the 18th BRICS Summit at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi on 12-13 September 2026 during its fourth BRICS Chairship (earlier: 2012, 2016, 2021). It marked 20 years of BRICS and concluded with more than 50 development-oriented outcomes. Theme: 'Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability'.
  • The leaders adopted the New Delhi Declaration by consensus on 12 September; it also strongly condemned the Pahalgam terror attack and backed a zero-tolerance approach to terrorism.
  • Highlights: continued support for UN reform; reports of the BRICS Business Council and Women's Business Alliance presented; a dedicated BRICS Women's Working Group; focus on a people-centric, youth-oriented BRICS and cultural diplomacy.
  • China will assume the BRICS Chairship for 2027.

EXAM POINT: BRICS has 11 members: Brazil, China, Egypt, Ethiopia, India, Indonesia, Iran, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa and UAE; partner countries: Belarus, Cuba, Bolivia, Kazakhstan, Malaysia, Nigeria, Thailand, Uganda, Uzbekistan, Vietnam. 26th SCO Summit, Bishkek - Bishkek Declaration Signed31 Aug - 1 Sep 2026

  • The 26th Meeting of the SCO Council of Heads of State was held in Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan, marking the 25th anniversary of the SCO (founded 2001). Theme: '25 Years of the SCO: Together for Sustainable Peace, Development and Prosperity'. Chair: Kyrgyz President Sadyr Japarov.
  • Outcomes: Bishkek Declaration, four MoUs and 20+ decisions/statements - on climate, nuclear safety, energy, drug trafficking, a Universal Centre for Countering Security Challenges and Threats, a Green Technology Corridor (2026-30) and an information security plan (2026-28).
  • India-backed initiatives included English as an official SCO language, Civilisation Dialogue Forum, Young Scientist Forum and Young Authors' Conclave. PM Modi spoke against terrorism as a 'strategic asset' and presented the SECURE vision.
  • Rotating chairmanship passed to Pakistan. The 25th Meeting of the SCO Council of Heads of Government (CHG) followed in Dushanbe, Tajikistan on 19-20 October 2026 (India represented by Defence Minister Rajnath Singh; PM Modi expected to join virtually).

EXAM POINT: SCO members (10): India, China, Russia, Pakistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, Iran, Belarus.

India Rejects Court of Arbitration Award on Indus Waters Treaty31 Aug - 1 Sep 2026

  • The Court of Arbitration (The Hague) issued an award on interim measures and the status of the Indus Waters Treaty, holding that India's grounds did not justify suspending or terminating the treaty and that it must keep observing its obligations.
  • It also restricted concreting of the dam wall and power intake of the 850-MW Ratle hydroelectric project (Chenab) until 90 days after the Neutral Expert's final decision, expected in July 2027.
  • The MEA called the body 'illegally constituted', said India never recognised it and that the treaty remains in abeyance - a decision taken on 23 April 2025 after the Pahalgam attack.

EXAM POINT: Indus Waters Treaty signed 19 September 1960 (India-Pakistan, World Bank as signatory for certain provisions). Eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) - India; Western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) - Pakistan.

Centre Notifies India's First Four 'Mega Ports'25 Sep 2026

  • The Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways, under Section 73 of the Indian Ports Act, 2025, notified Mundra, Deendayal (Kandla), Jawaharlal Nehru (Nhava Sheva) and Paradip as mega ports.
  • Criteria (notified 30 July 2026): bulk cargo port - at least 150 million tonnes; container port - at least 7.5 million TEUs in the previous financial year. Status is valid for five years and does not change a port's 'major' or 'non-major' classification.
  • Paradip (Odisha) is the only mega port on the east coast. Mundra is privately owned (Adani Ports) and classed as non-major. India has 14 notified major ports, 12 operational.

EXAM POINT: Indian Ports Act, 2025 replaced the Indian Ports Act, 1908.
